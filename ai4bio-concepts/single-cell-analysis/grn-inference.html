Core Concepts

What GRN inference is

A gene regulatory network (GRN) is a directed graph whose nodes are genes and whose edges represent the causal regulatory action of a regulator (typically a transcription factor) on a target. Inferring a GRN means estimating that directed graph from data — usually bulk or single-cell RNA-seq, optionally augmented by chromatin accessibility (scATAC-seq), TF-binding motifs, ChIP-seq, perturbation screens, and population genetics.

There are two fundamentally different ways to do this. The statistical route (SCENIC, GENIE3, GRNBoost, WGCNA-style methods) scores co-expression or motif-anchored TF–target relations and produces a confidence ranking. The causal route (NOTEARS family, DCDI, ENCO, MR via Airqtl) tries to recover an underlying directed acyclic graph that explains the joint distribution of expressions under interventions. The two approaches answer different questions: statistical methods say "which TFs explain this gene's expression", causal methods say "which edges, if reversed, would change downstream behaviour". For drug target nomination, you want the causal answer; for cell-type characterisation, the statistical one is often enough.

Why it matters for biology and target identification

  • Upstream regulators are candidate drug targets. If gene X consistently sits upstream of a disease-relevant program (e.g., the sepsis exhaustion signature, the T2D beta-cell dysfunction module), modulating X is the rational intervention. Geneformer’s cardiomyocyte demonstration (PLN, GSN), CellOracle’s Lhx1a discovery in zebrafish, and Ota et al. 2025’s identification of SUPT5H as the MCH master regulator all follow this logic.
  • Cell-type-specific GRNs reveal context-dependent biology. 84% of CD4+ T-cell regulatory edges differ between flu and mock conditions (Airqtl); same TF, different targets across cell states (scMTNI, SCENIC+). Static "tissue-level" GRNs miss this entirely.
  • Virtual knockout enables cheap hypothesis triage. scTenifoldKnk runs a whole-transcriptome KO in 4–5 days on a desktop; CellOracle simulates TF perturbation along a developmental trajectory; CellNavi predicts driver genes directly from a learned manifold. The bottleneck in target discovery is not data — it is testable hypotheses, and GRN-based KO is the cheapest hypothesis generator in biology.
  • The 2025 benchmarks have moved the goalposts. CausalBench (Replogle Perturb-seq, ~163K interventional cells) showed that most "interventional" causal-discovery methods do not in fact beat their observational counterparts on real CRISPRi data, and that acyclicity-enforcing methods are worse than feedback-permitting ones. The genome-scale CD4+ T-cell Perturb-seq (Zhu et al. 2025, 22M cells, 12,748 genes) makes this even sharper.

Why causality is hard

  • Observational identifiability ceiling. From expression data alone, you can recover the underlying DAG only up to its Markov equivalence class — many directions remain ambiguous (Sachs benchmark; NOTEARS-MLP recovers 3 reversed edges out of 13). Interventions (CRISPR-KO, CRISPRi, drug perturbation) break the symmetry.
  • Acyclicity is a lie. Real GRNs have feedback loops (NF-κB autoregulation, STAT1/IFN, p53/MDM2). Every DAG-enforcing method (NOTEARS, NOTEARS-MLP, DCDI, AnalyticDAG) starts by assuming this away. ENCO and CausalBench's top methods do not enforce acyclicity, and they win on real data.
  • Hidden confounders. Cell cycle, donor effects, batch effects, dropout patterns — all create non-causal correlations that look like edges. Mendelian-randomisation methods (Airqtl) use genetic instruments to dodge this; LINGER bakes manifold regularisation in; the Gene→Program→Trait paper uses LoF burden tests as an orthogonal directional signal.
  • Scale. NOTEARS is O(d³) per iteration — usable for d < ~500 genes. ENCO scales to 1,000 nodes in 9 hours. Whole-transcriptome (d ≈ 20K) causal discovery is still an open problem; current practice is to pre-filter to a candidate panel.

Evolution Timeline

2017 — SCENIC and the statistical era

SCENIC (Nat Methods) lays down the canonical TF–regulon pipeline: GENIE3/GRNBoost co-expression → RcisTarget motif filtering → AUCell regulon scoring. ARI > 0.80 for cell-type identification, 151 regulons from mouse brain, NFATC2 nominated as a melanoma invasion repressor.

2018–2020 — Continuous-optimisation DAG learning

NOTEARS (NeurIPS 2018) replaces combinatorial DAG search with a single smooth acyclicity constraint h(W) = tr(e^{W∘W}) − d = 0. NOTEARS-MLP (AISTATS 2020) extends to nonparametric SEMs via partial-derivative norms. DCDI (NeurIPS 2020) adds interventional data and normalising flows. The "causal discovery as gradient descent" paradigm is born.

2022 — ENCO and virtual knockouts

ENCO (ICLR) factors each edge into existence (γ) and orientation (θ), drops the acyclicity constraint, and scales to 1,000-node graphs in 9 hours — the first interventional causal-discovery method that runs at near-transcriptome scale. scTenifoldKnk (Patterns) shows you can do whole-genome in-silico KO on WT scRNA-seq alone, in ~1 minute per gene.

2023 — Multi-omic GRN: CellOracle, SCENIC+, scMTNI, LINGER

scATAC-seq + scRNA-seq integration becomes standard. CellOracle (Nature) couples a chromatin-derived base GRN with cluster-specific regression and signal-propagation simulation. SCENIC+ (Nat Methods) introduces 34,524 motifs, enhancer-driven GRNs, ARI = 1.00 on ENCODE cell lines. scMTNI adds multi-task learning over cell lineages. LINGER claims 4–7× accuracy gains via lifelong-learning regularisation. The 2023 Nat Rev Genet review synthesises 27+ methods.

2024–2025 — Benchmarks reset the field

GRouNdGAN (Nat Commun 2024) finally aligns simulator-benchmark rankings with experimental rankings. CausalBench (Commun Biol 2025, K562/RPE1 Perturb-seq) reveals that most "interventional" methods barely beat observational ones, and that acyclicity-enforcing methods underperform. AnalyticDAG (ICLR 2025) unifies NOTEARS/DAGMA/polynomial constraints. scMINER, dGCNA, and CellNavi push the frontier in different directions: MI-based clustering with hidden-driver inference, differential coordination networks, and deep-learning driver-gene prediction (AUROC 0.874 vs SCENIC 0.596).

2025 — Causal integration and population scale

Airqtl (Nat Commun) achieves >10⁸ speedup for single-cell eQTL mapping, enabling Mendelian-randomisation cGRNs validated against CRISPR at Pearson r = 0.82. The Gene→Program→Trait paper (Nature) integrates LoF burden tests (UKB n = 454,787) with K562 Perturb-seq, predicting MCH direction for 43/59 top genes. Genome-scale CD4+ T-cell Perturb-seq (22M cells, 12,748 genes, 3 stim conditions) becomes the new gold-standard substrate for causal GRN inference in primary cells.

Side-by-Side: Co-Expression + Motif vs Chromatin-Anchored + Perturbation Simulation

SCENIC (2017) and CellOracle (2023) are the two most-cited GRN pipelines in single-cell biology. They separate on the single most important design axis: what evidence defines a TF→target edge — co-expression importance ranking (statistical) versus open-chromatin TF-binding + ridge regression + signal propagation (causal-simulatable).

The split traces back to one root decision — what counts as evidence for an edge: SCENIC trusts gene-expression co-variation (with a motif plausibility filter), so it works on scRNA-seq alone and outputs regulon activity scores useful for clustering; CellOracle demands open chromatin as a structural prerequisite, so it requires paired scATAC-seq, but in return it can simulate the transcriptome-wide effect of knocking out any TF, enabling causal hypothesis generation that SCENIC cannot support.

Method Families

Classical TF–Target Inference (5)

Statistical methods that rank TF–target relationships from co-expression, mutual information, or differential coordination — usually with a motif-enrichment filter to remove indirect edges.

SCENIC: single-cell regulatory network inference and clustering

2017 Nat Methods Co-expression + motif

SCENIC is the canonical scRNA-seq GRN pipeline. It chains GENIE3/GRNBoost random-forest co-expression with RcisTarget motif enrichment (NES > 3.0) and AUCell regulon scoring, identifying robust cell-state regulons even with high dropout, transcriptional bursting, and across batches.

  • Cell-type identification ARI > 0.80, sensitivity 0.88, specificity 0.99 on mouse brain
  • 151 regulons retained from 1,046 GENIE3 co-expression modules (7% pass motif filter)
  • Identifies rare populations with 2–6 cells; cross-species DLX1/2 regulon conserved in mouse and human brain
  • Nominates NFATC2 (114 targets) and NFIB as melanoma invasion repressors — validated by IHC and KD
  • Scales via GRNBoost to 1.3M cells (mouse embryonic brain, 10X Chromium)

SCENIC+: single-cell multiomic inference of enhancers and gene regulatory networks

2023 Nat Methods Enhancer-driven GRN

SCENIC+ extends SCENIC to paired scRNA + scATAC. It uses pycisTopic for unsupervised enhancer discovery, pycisTarget with the largest motif collection to date (34,524 motifs in 1,986 clusters), and GRNBoost2 + GSEA-based eRegulon refinement to produce TF–enhancer–gene triplets with cell-state resolution.

  • ARI = 1.00 separating 8 ENCODE cell lines on eRegulon scores; competitors mix lines
  • 178 TFs identified on ENCODE; highest F1 vs CellOracle, Pando, FigR, GRaNIE, SCENIC
  • 402,838 region–gene links; correlation r = 0.25 with Hi-C (best among methods); predicted expression r = 0.61
  • SOX10-knockdown simulation matches experimental MEL→MES transition RNA-seq in melanoma
  • Cross-species cortex: 60 conserved TFs, 28% conserved targets, 6% conserved regions — TF lexicon is preserved while enhancers turn over

scMINER: a mutual-information framework for clustering and hidden driver inference

2025 Nat Commun MI clustering + SJARACNe

scMINER unifies mutual-information cell clustering (MICA) with MI-based network inference (SJARACNe, an scRNA-tuned ARACNe variant) and activity estimation for both ~1,500 TFs and ~5,500 signalling proteins. It is the first scRNA framework to systematically infer signalling-protein networks in addition to TF regulons.

  • Average clustering ARI 0.84 vs 0.69–0.78 for Seurat, Scanpy, SC3s, scVI, scDeepCluster
  • Network inference AUROC 0.69–0.77 on CROP-seq validation; 94% TF-driver agreement with SCENIC
  • Distinguishes CD4 Treg vs CD4 TCM where linear-correlation methods fail
  • Inference scales from hundreds to 650K+ cells; ATAC/CROP/CITE validation across 5 datasets

Single-cell mRNA-regulation analysis reveals cell type-specific mechanisms of type 2 diabetes (dGCNA)

2025 Nat Commun Differential coordination network

dGCNA (differential Gene Coordination Network Analysis) builds gene–gene correlation networks separately for disease vs healthy single cells, then extracts modules of differentially coordinated genes via bootstrap thresholding (512 permutations) and WGCNA-style TOMdist clustering. Applied to T2D pancreatic islets, it uncovers 11 beta-cell modules even where DESeq2 fails.

  • 26% gene overlap between independent T2D datasets vs near-zero for DESeq2 — reproducibility advantage
  • Identifies CEBPG and TMEM176A/B as novel beta-cell dysfunction regulators
  • T2D-GWAS enrichment in the "Insulin Secretion" module: odds ratio 129 (p-adj = 6.08e-8)
  • Detects genes with unchanged expression but altered coordination — an axis DE misses

Scoring gene importance by interpreting single-cell foundation models (SIGnature)

2026 Nat Biotechnol Attribution-based TF ranking

SIGnature applies gradient-based attribution methods from explainable AI (Integrated Gradients, Input×Gradient, DeepLIFT via Captum) to scRNA-seq foundation models, quantifying each gene's contribution to a cell's position in the FM's latent embedding. Crucially, attributions rank transcription factors and marker genes significantly higher than log-normalised expression does — and demote ribosomal/mitochondrial artefacts — recovering lineage TFs that expression-level analysis systematically misses.

  • Lineage TFs GATA3/RORC/FOXP3 rank significantly higher by attribution in the correct T-cell subsets (Th2/Th17/Treg) vs expression (two-sided Wilcoxon P < 0.01)
  • Attribution-based NMF yields a Treg regulatory program consistent across 3 studies; FOXP3 is a top gene in 98% of attribution configs vs only 4% for expression
  • Attributions correlate far less with per-cell UMI counts than expression; top genes 93% stable under 50% dropout — robust to technical artefacts that confound co-expression networks
  • Attribution-based signature scoring tops F1 in 23 of 32 tasks, beating ANS, Scanpy, UCell, JASMINE, and mean expression across studies
  • MS1 monocyte signature queried across ~22 million cells / 412 studies in minutes; identifies MS1 activation in Kawasaki disease, SFTS, and HLH — validated in vitro (KD serum induces MS1; IVIG reduces it)

Causal Discovery / DAG-based (5)

Continuous-optimisation methods that recover a directed acyclic graph from observational and/or interventional data via smooth acyclicity constraints. Theoretically appealing, but the 2025 CausalBench results show feedback-permitting methods often beat them on real Perturb-seq.

DAGs with NO TEARS: Continuous Optimization for Structure Learning

2018 NeurIPS Smooth DAG constraint

NOTEARS reformulates the NP-hard combinatorial DAG-learning problem as a smooth equality-constrained continuous program by introducing the algebraic identity h(W) = tr(e^{W∘W}) − d = 0 ↔ W is acyclic. Combined with an ℓ&sub1;-penalised least-squares loss, structure learning becomes a black-box optimisation solvable in ~50 lines of Python via augmented Lagrangian + L-BFGS.

  • First fully continuous DAG-learning formulation — replaces combinatorial parent-set search
  • Lower SHD than FGS on dense/scale-free graphs; within ~2 score units of GOBNILP exact ILP at d=10
  • ~50-line reference implementation in PyTorch; recovers structure AND edge weights
  • Linear SEM only; O(d³) matrix-exponential cost limits to ~few hundred nodes
Paper Code

Learning Sparse Nonparametric DAGs (NOTEARS-MLP)

2020 AISTATS Nonparametric DAG

NOTEARS-MLP generalises the trace-exponential acyclicity constraint from linear SEMs to arbitrary differentiable models via partial-derivative norms: [W(f)]_kj = &lVert;∂_k f_j&rVert;_L². Proposition 1 proves that for an MLP, this reduces to first-layer input-column norms regardless of depth — a clean, depth-independent encoding of functional dependency.

  • Best SHD across MLP / index / additive-GP nonlinear settings on ER and SF graphs (d up to 40)
  • Sachs flow-cytometry (n=7466, d=11): SHD = 16 vs NOTEARS-linear 22, GNN 19, GSGES infeasible
  • NOTEARS-Sob (Sobolev basis) wins on additive-GP data; ++ pre/post-processing variants match CAM
  • Generic — any differentiable f_j works; avoids O(n³) kernel costs of GSGES

Differentiable Causal Discovery from Interventional Data (DCDI)

2020 NeurIPS Interventional + flows

DCDI is the first general continuous-constrained framework that leverages observational + interventional data with normalising-flow density models. It jointly learns a binary adjacency mask, per-variable conditional densities (Gaussian or deep sigmoidal flow), and optionally the intervention-target matrix — with formal identifiability guarantees for known and unknown intervention targets.

  • Outperforms GIES, CAM, IGSP, UT-IGSP on dense 20-node graphs across linear/ANM/NN mechanisms
  • Only nonlinear method scaling via SGD to 100 nodes × 1M samples
  • Theorem 2: identifiability with unknown intervention targets given an ℓ&sub1; penalty on the intervention set
  • DCDI-DSF (deep sigmoidal flows) handles multi-modal noise where DCDI-G fails
Paper Code

Efficient Neural Causal Discovery without Acyclicity Constraints (ENCO)

2022 ICLR Edge existence + orientation

ENCO factors each potential edge into two independent sigmoid probabilities — γ_ij for existence (updated from obs + intervention) and θ_ij for orientation (θ_ij = −θ_ji, updated only under interventions on X_i or X_j). The orientation parameter automatically prevents 2-cycles, so no acyclicity regulariser is needed — enabling near-transcriptome-scale convergence.

  • Scales to 1,000-node graphs in 9h with 1 missing edge out of 1M possible — DCDI OOM at this scale
  • SHD ≤ 0.2 on 25-node bidiag/chain/random where DCDI and SDI sit at 5–17
  • 96.8% confounder recall, 100% confounder precision on latent-confounder detection via Eq. 8 split-γ score
  • Low-variance gradient estimator: difference of two MC estimates, ~10× lower std-dev than Bengio 2020
  • Recovers BnLearn cancer / asia / sachs / child / pigs with zero errors

Analytic DAG Constraints for Differentiable DAG Learning

2025 ICLR Unified DAG framework

AnalyticDAG proves that any analytic f in the class F = {c&sub0; + Σc_i x^i | c_i > 0, lim c_i/c_{i+1} > 0} evaluated on a non-negative weighted adjacency yields a valid DAG constraint tr[f(B˜)] = c&sub0;d. F is closed under differentiation/addition/multiplication, so NOTEARS, DAGMA, and Zhang 2022 all become special cases, and higher-order constraints with stronger gradients can be derived systematically.

  • Unifies matrix-exponential (NOTEARS), log-det (DAGMA), and polynomial (Zhang 2022) DAG constraints
  • Higher-order constraints have gradients that GROW with order, mitigating gradient vanishing
  • Geometric power-series structure permits O(log t) evaluation via repeated squaring
  • ER1-Gaussian d=1000: Order-4 SHD 389.4 vs DAGMA 588.8, NOTEARS-exp 488.6, PC 563.9
  • Sachs: SHD/CPDAG = 13/9 across Order-1..4 vs Original GraN-DAG 15/10

Multi-omic Neural-Network GRN Inference (4)

Methods that jointly use scRNA-seq + scATAC-seq (and external bulk atlases) to infer cell-state-specific GRNs with neural-network or probabilistic-graphical-model backbones. The current default for "build me a usable, cell-type-specific GRN".

Dissecting cell identity via network inference and in silico gene perturbation (CellOracle)

2023 Nature Base-GRN + simulation

CellOracle integrates scATAC-seq-derived base GRNs (TF-motif scans on accessible chromatin) with cluster-specific Bayesian-ridge regression on scRNA-seq, then simulates TF perturbations via iterative signal propagation. The result is a vector-field representation that predicts cell-fate changes from a TF knockdown without requiring perturbation training data.

  • GRN inference AUROC 0.73–0.91 (scATAC base) vs ChIP-seq ground truth in haematopoiesis
  • Correctly predicts 12/12 known TF-KO phenotypes in mouse haematopoiesis
  • Zebrafish noto KO: predicted +38.6% prechordal plate expansion (P = 1.07×10⁻⁴), validated experimentally
  • Lhx1a discovery: previously unreported axial mesoderm regulator validated by crispants with quantitative cluster shifts
  • Captures stage-specific Tal1 function: essential early, dispensable late in erythroid differentiation

Inference of cell type-specific gene regulatory networks on cell lineages (scMTNI)

2023 Nat Commun Multi-task lineage GRN

scMTNI is a probabilistic graphical model that jointly infers cell-type-specific GRNs along a cell-lineage tree by sharing information across related cell types. It combines scRNA-seq + scATAC-seq priors with a lineage-tree prior modelling edge gain/loss dynamics, applicable to both linear and branching topologies and to regulators without known motifs (e.g., signalling proteins).

  • AUPR ~0.23–0.27 and F-score 0.2–0.3 on simulated data — best among multi-task methods
  • Multi-task learning consistently outperforms single-task algorithms across cell numbers 200–2000
  • MEF→iPSC reprogramming: identifies Esrrb-centered network in pluripotency-committed cells
  • Detects MEF-specific regulons (Aebp1, Loxl2) that persist in stalled (non-reprogrammed) cells
  • Edge clustering + topic modelling reveal subnetwork rewiring across lineage

Inferring gene regulatory networks from single-cell multiome data using atlas-scale external data (LINGER)

2024 Nat Biotechnol Lifelong-learning NN

LINGER is a lifelong-learning neural network that pre-trains on 201 ENCODE bulk RNA-ATAC samples, then refines on single-cell multiome via elastic weight consolidation (Fisher-information regularisation) and TF–RE motif Laplacian regularisation. GRN edges are extracted via Shapley-value attribution for interpretability.

  • 4–7× relative AUC and AUPR-ratio improvement over SCENIC+, GENIE3, PIDC on trans-regulation (20 ChIP-seq sets)
  • Higher cis-regulation AUC vs scNN across all distance bins (0–3kb to 100–200kb)
  • Two-round lifelong pre-training (ENCODE1+ENCODE2+sc) beats single-round on 85.5% of ChIP-seq datasets
  • Robust across metacell aggregation methods (SEACells vs original, P = 0.89)
  • Shapley-based interpretation enables disease-variant attribution

CellNavi predicts genes directing cellular transitions by learning a gene graph-enhanced cell state manifold

2025 Nat Cell Biol GeneGraph transformer

CellNavi is a two-component deep-learning framework: a Cell Manifold Model (6-layer transformer with custom GeneGraph attention) pretrained on 20M Human Cell Atlas single cells with 20M NicheNet-derived directional gene graphs, and a Driver Gene Predictor (2-layer MLP) fine-tuned on CRISPR screens. Identifies driver genes even when the perturbed gene's expression is masked from input.

  • Schmidt primary T-cell dataset: AUROC 0.874 vs SCENIC 0.596, GEARS 0.511, DGE 0.617
  • Norman K562 dataset: AUROC 0.888; Top-1 accuracy 0.621, Top-5 accuracy 0.733
  • Double perturbations: ranks 1st and 2nd perturbed genes at avg 7.9 / 31.2 out of 105 candidates
  • GeneGraph attention uses centrality (in/out-degree) and shortest-path-distance encodings
  • SCENIC fails on single-cell driver prediction: 61.6% of samples have no regulon found, 58.3% candidates excluded

Perturbation Benchmarks & Simulators (4)

The empirical foundation of modern GRN inference. BEELINE established the modern benchmarking framework and its real-vs-synthetic ranking mismatch; GRouNdGAN is the one simulator later shown to resolve that mismatch; CausalBench and the Zhu 2025 CD4+ Perturb-seq atlas are the real-data benchmarks that reset expectations for interventional methods.

Benchmarking algorithms for gene regulatory network inference from single-cell transcriptomic data (BEELINE)

2020 Nat Methods Benchmarking framework

BEELINE is the paper that defined how GRN-inference methods should be benchmarked: it evaluates a dozen algorithms (including GENIE3, GRNBoost2, SCODE, PIDC, SINCERITIES, and PPCOR) on both synthetic data generated from curated Boolean models of known regulatory circuits and real scRNA-seq datasets with literature-curated ground-truth networks (ChIP-seq, STRING, non-specific). Its central, field-shaping finding is that method rankings on synthetic data do not transfer to real data — no single method is consistently best across both regimes, and even the strongest performers barely beat random on real datasets by AUPRC. This real-vs-synthetic ranking mismatch is the exact problem GRouNdGAN was later built to solve, and the BEELINE curated ground-truth networks remain the standard evaluation substrate cited throughout this module.

  • First systematic, reproducible benchmarking pipeline comparing GRN-inference algorithms on matched synthetic and real datasets
  • Shows synthetic-data method rankings (from Boolean-model simulators) do not predict real-data rankings — the core motivation for GRouNdGAN's causal-GAN approach
  • All evaluated methods perform close to random by AUPRC against literature-curated ground truth on real scRNA-seq, exposing how weak the field's ground-truth signal actually is
  • Establishes the curated Boolean-model synthetic networks and literature ground-truth networks still used as reference benchmarks by later work (GRouNdGAN, CausalBench)

GRouNdGAN: GRN-guided simulation of single-cell RNA-seq data using causal generative adversarial networks

2024 Nat Commun Causal GAN simulator

GRouNdGAN is a causal Wasserstein-GAN that imposes a user-specified GRN as an architectural constraint: a pretrained "causal controller" generates realistic TF expression, then per-target generators receive only their regulating TFs (per GRN adjacency). The output is statistically indistinguishable from real cells and faithful to the encoded regulatory graph — enabling gold-standard GRN-inference benchmarks and in-silico KO via interventional sampling.

  • First simulator whose GRN-inference method ranking matches BEELINE experimental benchmarks
  • PBMC-CTL: best across cosine (0.00057), MMD (0.026), RF AUROC (0.54), miLISI (1.89)
  • In-silico TF KO produces significant DEG changes in 66.5% of GRN-encoded edges
  • GRNBoost2/PIDC/GENIE3 achieve AUPRC 0.68 on imposed edges vs 0.12 on unimposed (near-random)
  • Preserves trajectories: PAGA topology matches real BoneMarrow data
Paper Code

A large-scale benchmark for network inference from single-cell perturbation data (CausalBench)

2025 Commun Biol Real-data benchmark

CausalBench is the first large-scale real-data interventional benchmark for causal GRN inference. It evaluates 17+ methods on Replogle 2022 genome-wide Perturb-seq (K562 and RPE1, ~163K cells each, 622/383 perturbations) using both biological precision/recall against cell-line-filtered CORUM/STRING/ChIP-seq and statistical Wasserstein + False Omission Rate metrics.

  • Top F1: Guanlab 0.223/0.257, Mean Difference 0.168/0.240, GRNBoost 0.175/0.069 on K562/RPE1
  • Most "interventional" methods barely beat their observational counterparts — even GIES ≤ GES
  • DAG-enforcing methods (NOTEARS-MLP F1 0.121/0.068) underperform feedback-permitting methods
  • Only SparseRC satisfies graph + sample + intervention scaling axes simultaneously (lower absolute performance)
  • Methods plateau in performance at ~50% of available samples — opens room for sample-efficient methods

Genome-scale Perturb-seq in primary human CD4+ T cells maps context-specific regulators

2025 bioRxiv Primary-cell Perturb-seq

A genome-scale CRISPRi Perturb-seq screen across 22M primary human CD4+ T cells from 4 donors and 3 stimulation conditions (Rest, Stim8hr, Stim48hr). Targets all 12,748 expressed genes with 26,504 gRNAs, profiled via 10x Flex probe-based scRNA-seq (~10,000 UMIs/cell). The first genome-scale perturbation atlas in primary cells — the new GRN-benchmark substrate.

  • 33.4M high-quality transcriptomes; 21.99M cells with single-guide assignment (65.8%); 73% on-target KD
  • 2,035,311 significant regulator→gene effects across 4 donors and 3 conditions
  • ~1/3 of regulator clusters show condition-specific effects — same regulator, different targets across states
  • 1,556 perturbations affect ≥ 1 of 30 cytokines; identifies NFKB2, KDM1A as divergent IL10/IL21 regulators
  • Links T-cell aging signature (TSC1/2, GATOR, UBA2/UBE2I) to OneK1K population-scale variation

Virtual Knockout & Genetics-Anchored GRN (3)

Methods that exploit external causal signal — a hand-built KO ablation, natural genetic variation (Mendelian randomisation), or loss-of-function burden tests — to anchor GRN inference in something stronger than co-expression.

scTenifoldKnk: an efficient virtual knockout tool for gene function predictions

2022 Patterns In-silico KO

scTenifoldKnk builds a denoised WT GRN via PC-regression + CP tensor decomposition, generates a pseudo-KO GRN by zeroing the outgoing-edge row of the target gene, then compares the two via quasi-manifold alignment with a chi-squared test for significant differential regulation. Whole-transcriptome KO at ~1 minute per gene on a desktop CPU.

  • Recapitulates real-animal Nkx2-1, Trem2, Hnf4a/g, Cftr, Dmd, Mecp2 KO findings using WT scRNA-seq only
  • Trem2 KO microglia: 128 DR genes enriched for Alzheimer disease, lysosome, TYROBP, LDL/HDL/TG metabolism
  • Mecp2 KO neurons: 211 shared DR genes (p < 10⁻⁵) across replicates, ρ = 0.68 ranking concordance
  • ~1 min/gene; 6,000-gene genome-wide KO in 4–5 days on a single i7 + 16 GB RAM

Gene regulatory network inference in the era of single-cell multi-omics

2023 Nat Rev Genet Review

Comprehensive review of 30+ multimodal GRN methods (SCENIC+, CellOracle, FigR, GRaNIE, ANANSE, Pando, scMEGA, IReNA, Dictys, scMTNI, etc.), organised around a three-step modelling decomposition — (1) gene-expression prediction from TF expression, (2) TF–CRE assignment via motif scanning on accessible peaks, (3) CRE–gene linkage via distance or Hi-C — plus a group/trajectory/hybrid/contrast taxonomy for capturing dynamic and condition-specific regulatory rewiring. Reinforces the chromatin-first design principle: filtering TF–gene candidates by chromatin accessibility removes biologically implausible edges where the regulator cannot physically bind.

  • 27+ tools tabulated by data-type requirements, statistical framework (frequentist vs Bayesian), and CRE–gene distance cutoffs (10kb–1Mb) — validated edges concentrate < 100 kb
  • Disease applications: Alzheimer's (ZEB1/MAFB), COVID-19 fibroblasts, immunotherapy resistance in T cells; Brachyury/mouse organogenesis case study links GRN priming to developmental fate
  • Documents that chromatin accessibility significantly reduces false-positive TF–gene edges; motif-database choice (JASPAR/HOCOMOCO/CIS-BP) and matcher (MOODs/FIMO/HOMER) introduce substantial heterogeneity
  • Frames TF activity inference as the downstream layer (GSEA, AUCell, VIPER) and the missing-benchmark problem that CausalBench and GRouNdGAN later solve

Reading Order for Newcomers

1. SCENIC (2017, Nat Methods)

The pipeline every GRN paper compares to. GENIE3 + RcisTarget + AUCell. Understand this and you understand the statistical-GRN era.

2. Nat Rev Genet 2023 review

Field synthesis covering 30+ multimodal methods. Read before deciding which tool to run — it will save you from rebuilding a GRaNIE-style pipeline that already exists in SCENIC+.

3. NOTEARS (2018, NeurIPS) → ENCO (2022, ICLR)

The arc from combinatorial → continuous → constraint-free DAG learning. Read NOTEARS to understand the acyclicity trick; read ENCO to understand why you should not enforce it.

4. CellOracle (2023, Nature)

The canonical "multi-omic GRN → in-silico perturbation" pipeline. The Lhx1a zebrafish discovery is the cleanest published example of a useful target-ID hypothesis from a GRN.

5. CausalBench (2025, Commun Biol)

The reality check. Most "interventional" methods barely beat observational ones on real CRISPRi data; DAG enforcement hurts; only Mean Difference / Guanlab / SparseRC are SOTA. Read before believing any new causal-discovery paper's headline.

6. Gene→Program→Trait (2025, Nature) + Airqtl (2025, Nat Commun)

Where the field is heading: integrate Perturb-seq with orthogonal causal signals (LoF burden, MR via sceQTLs). The 6.3× LoF-enrichment for HBA1 regulators and Airqtl's STAT1 r = 0.82 vs CRISPR are the two cleanest demonstrations that genetic anchoring works.

Practical Implementation Guide

If you have observational scRNA-seq only (no perturbation, no ATAC)

Run SCENIC (or pySCENIC + GRNBoost) for a fast TF-regulon baseline and use AUCell scores for downstream clustering / pseudo-bulk. Add scMINER if you also need signalling-protein activity inference. Use scTenifoldKnk for cheap whole-transcriptome in-silico KO — it requires only WT scRNA-seq and runs in ~1 minute per gene. For disease-vs-control comparisons, dGCNA is more reproducible than DESeq2-style DE on single cells.

If you have paired scRNA + scATAC multiome

This is now the default. SCENIC+ for enhancer-driven GRNs with the largest motif collection (34,524 motifs), CellOracle if you specifically want in-silico TF perturbation simulation along a developmental trajectory, scMTNI if your data has a clear lineage tree, and LINGER if you have an external bulk atlas (ENCODE-like) you want to pre-train on for 4–7× accuracy gain. All four read the same paired-multiome inputs — run multiple and intersect.

If you have Perturb-seq or other interventional data

This is the only data type where causal discovery actually delivers. Start with the CausalBench top performers (Mean Difference, Guanlab, SparseRC) — per the 2025 benchmark, they substantially outperform classical methods including NOTEARS-MLP and SCENIC on K562/RPE1. For driver-gene ID at the single-perturbation level, CellNavi reaches AUROC 0.874 vs SCENIC 0.596. Use GRouNdGAN-simulated data with a known imposed GRN as your gold-standard benchmark before reporting on real data — it is the only simulator whose method ranking matches BEELINE.

If you want to integrate GRN with foundation models / population genetics

This is where the leverage is for drug-target discovery. CellNavi's GeneGraph attention is a clean recipe for injecting GRN structure into a transformer cell-state model. For trait-anchored targets, follow the Gene→Program→Trait recipe: cNMF programs → LoF burden tests → regulator–burden correlation. If you have population-scale scRNA-seq with genotypes, Airqtl's MR framework gives genetic-instrument-validated cGRN edges in ~10 minutes per dataset, with external CRISPR validation at r = 0.82.

Common Pitfalls

  • Co-expression ≠ regulation. Co-expression networks (GENIE3, GRNBoost, ARACNe, WGCNA) confound direct TF→target effects with downstream co-regulation, batch effects, cell-cycle confounding, and reverse causation. Always layer in either a motif-anchored filter (RcisTarget, SCENIC+) or an interventional anchor (Airqtl MR, CRISPR perturbation) before nominating a target.
  • Acyclicity is a modelling lie. Real GRNs have feedback loops (NF-κB autoregulation, p53/MDM2, STAT/IFN). NOTEARS, NOTEARS-MLP, DCDI, AnalyticDAG all assume this away. CausalBench shows acyclicity-enforcing methods underperform feedback-permitting methods on real Perturb-seq. Use DAG methods on small candidate subnetworks; never on whole-transcriptome biological data without a feedback-tolerant alternative.
  • "Interventional" does not mean better. CausalBench shows that GIES, DCDI-G, DCDI-DSF, DCDFG-MLP, Catran often do not beat their observational counterparts on Replogle Perturb-seq. Only Mean Difference, Guanlab, SparseRC, and Betterboost show meaningful uplift from interventional data. Demand a CausalBench-style evaluation, not a synthetic-data win.
  • Synthetic-data benchmarks lie systematically. SERGIO, BoolODE, scDESIGN2-style simulators produce method rankings that do not match experimental rankings — with one exception, GRouNdGAN. If your evaluation rests on a single simulator, you probably picked the one your method wins on.
  • Foundation-model GRNs are not free. Geneformer, scGPT, and other scFMs claim implicit GRN knowledge via attention heads, but the 2024 SSL benchmark and CausalBench together show linear baselines often match them on real perturbation prediction. Use GRNs as an interpretable structural prior on top of foundation embeddings, not as a substitute for them.
  • Tissue-level GRNs are misleading. 84% of CD4+ T-cell edges differ between flu and mock condition (Airqtl); 60% of melanoma regulators are state-specific (SCENIC+). A single static GRN per "tissue" hides exactly the biology you care about for therapeutic targets. Always infer per cell-type, per condition.

Hands-On Practice

The fastest path from raw count matrix to an interpretable GRN is the pySCENIC / arboreto stack: run GRNBoost2 to score all TF–target pairs by co-expression importance, then layer on cisTarget motif enrichment to filter indirect edges. The walkthrough below follows that exact path so you can reproduce the core SCENIC step on any scRNA-seq dataset in under an hour.

Environment & packages

Install into a dedicated conda environment; arboreto pulls in dask for distributed GRNBoost2, and pyscenic brings its own pinned loom/anndata stack that can conflict with a general scanpy environment.

pip install celloracle           # in-silico TF perturbation + fate simulation

Hardware. GRNBoost2 with dask scales linearly with cores; a 16-core workstation handles ~5,000 cells × 2,000 TFs in ~15 minutes. For datasets > 50,000 cells or full TF lists (~1,500 TFs), request an HPC node with 32+ cores and 128 GB RAM — GRNBoost2 is memory-bound, not GPU-bound.

Data structures & formats

  • AnnData (scanpy): primary container — adata.X holds the log-normalised count matrix (cells × genes); adata.var_names must be gene symbols matching the TF list.
  • TF list: a plain Python list of gene symbols (e.g., allTFs_hg38.txt from the pySCENIC resources page); used to restrict GRNBoost2 regulators.
  • Adjacency table: a pandas DataFrame with columns TF, target, importance — the raw GRNBoost2 output before motif filtering.
  • Regulons: pySCENIC Regulon objects — a TF plus its motif-enriched target set and NES score; stored as JSON or directly in AnnData.uns.
  • cisTarget motif-ranking databases (.feather): precomputed rankings of 20,000+ motifs over all human/mouse gene promoters; downloaded once (~3–10 GB) from the aertslab resource server.
  • Base GRN (CellOracle): a binary pd.DataFrame (TF × gene) derived from scATAC-seq peak–motif scanning; used as the structural prior before ridge-regression fitting.

Minimal code walkthrough

Load a preprocessed AnnData, run GRNBoost2 via arboreto with a human TF list to obtain an importance-ranked adjacency dataframe, then threshold and inspect the top edges.

import scanpy as sc
import pandas as pd
from arboreto.algo import grnboost2
from arboreto.utils import load_tf_names
from distributed import Client, LocalCluster

# 1. Load preprocessed single-cell data (log-normalised, HVGs selected)
adata = sc.read_h5ad("pbmc_3k_processed.h5ad")

# 2. Load TF list (download from: resources.aertslab.org/cistarget/tf_lists/)
tf_names = load_tf_names("allTFs_hg38.txt")
# Keep only TFs that are actually expressed in this dataset
tf_names = [tf for tf in tf_names if tf in adata.var_names]
print(f"Using {len(tf_names)} TFs out of {adata.n_vars} genes")

# 3. Build expression matrix (cells x genes) as a DataFrame
ex_matrix = pd.DataFrame(
    adata.X.toarray() if hasattr(adata.X, "toarray") else adata.X,
    index=adata.obs_names,
    columns=adata.var_names,
)

# 4. Run GRNBoost2 with a local dask cluster (adjust n_workers to your CPU count)
cluster = LocalCluster(n_workers=8, threads_per_worker=1)
client = Client(cluster)

adjacencies = grnboost2(
    expression_data=ex_matrix,
    tf_names=tf_names,
    client_or_address=client,
    seed=42,
)
client.close(); cluster.close()

# 5. Inspect top edges
adjacencies = adjacencies.sort_values("importance", ascending=False)
print(adjacencies.head(20))

# 6. Threshold: keep edges above the 95th-percentile importance score
threshold = adjacencies["importance"].quantile(0.95)
top_edges = adjacencies[adjacencies["importance"] >= threshold]
print(f"Top-5% edges: {len(top_edges)} TF–target pairs retained")

# Next step: feed `adjacencies` into pyscenic.prune.prune2df()
# with your downloaded .feather cisTarget rankings to filter by motif enrichment

Common pitfalls & tips

  • GRN inference is correlational, not causal. GRNBoost2 ranks pairs by co-expression importance; high importance means the TF's expression predicts the target's, not that it drives it. Always layer on motif filtering (RcisTarget / pySCENIC) or an external causal anchor (Airqtl MR, CRISPR data) before nominating a target for follow-up.
  • Cell number matters. SCENIC and GRNBoost2 need enough cells to estimate robust co-expression; < 500 cells per cell type typically yields noisy, low-recall regulons. Aggregate rare cell types across samples or use scMTNI's lineage sharing before running per-cluster inference.
  • Dropout inflates false negatives. Highly sparse genes will appear uncorrelated even when regulated. Use log-normalised counts (not raw UMIs) and consider imputation (MAGIC, ALRA) for the expression input — but benchmark the effect on your validation set first.
  • TF list quality is a hidden confounder. TFs not in your list are silently omitted from all edges; TFs in the list but not expressed add noise. Always intersect the canonical TF list with expressed genes (> 5% cells) before passing to GRNBoost2.
  • Motif-database genome version must match your reference. A hg19 cisTarget database used with hg38-mapped counts will miss or mis-map thousands of promoter regions. Check the filename for the genome build (e.g., hg38_500bp_up_100bp_down) and confirm it matches your alignment.
  • Validate against ChIP-seq or perturbation ground truth. AUROC on random held-out edges is uninformative — any method can score well. Use a cell-line-matched ChIP-seq compendium (ENCODE, ReMap) or a CRISPRi/KO experiment as your positive-edge set, as done in LINGER, CausalBench, and scMINER.
  • Runtime: use a dask cluster. GRNBoost2 on a full TF list (~1,500 TFs) × ~20,000 targets × 10,000 cells takes ~2 hours single-threaded. A LocalCluster(n_workers=16) brings this to ~10 minutes. For HPCC jobs, request a full node and set n_workers to the core count minus 2 for the scheduler overhead.
